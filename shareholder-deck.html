    Shareholder pack¶

    A briefing on the business, assembled from the company's own operating data and skinned as a 16-bit management game. Twenty-nine slides: the business in brief (with the network map), the year's numbers and the quarters behind them, the storm week that broke Paris, the Q4 decline the storm does not explain, the customer review, the three service levels behind that mix, the executive group rebuilt in the deck's own artwork, one slide for each seat — finance, operations, revenue, customer experience, fleet, people and data — the argument for engineering that context at all, the case for the team that already owns that data, a slide on what that shift looks like in practice — from analytics engineer to context engineer — a slide showing how every number traces back to the system that recorded it, the whole stack that produces it, a slide on the process — how a question becomes an answer — and the functions it calls, a closing audit of where those numbers can, and cannot, be trusted, and finally what that warehouse makes possible: the support-case agent, and where else agents slot in. The last four slides are blank templates — two in a title-and-body layout and two title-and-two-column — kept in the file so a hand-written slide can be dropped in by editing slides/presentation.html.

    The dataset is synthetic

    Jaffle Logistics is a worked example, not a real carrier. Nothing here is a real company's numbers, and no market or competitor data was available to the analysis — so the deck makes no claim about rivals or market share.
