The model ranks what it is given. Deciding what matters is the job.
A company has far more context than any question needs, and most of it is noise for that question. Jaffle Werke alone has 181 artifacts tied to it by an exact identifier: 144 of them support tickets. Eight of them tell the story of 2025: the contract, the storm, the Amsterdam handling pattern, the Q4 root-cause review. Ask the knowledge base "late deliveries and what is driving them" with raw vector search and one of the top ten is useful. The top hit is a three-word trailing sentence, "Reviewed with dispatch.", because short, formulaic text sits near the centre of embedding space and scores well against anything. Add a classification, a business taxonomy a person designed (account assessment, contract reference, weather disruption, vehicle or driver incident, handling or warehouse error, routine status), filter on it, and all ten are real account-level content. This is not a step you hand to the AI. The labels are applied by classify(), but the same unchanged text has landed in different categories between runs, and the taxonomy was designed for these questions, not handed down. Choosing what floats to the top, and checking it still does, is the context engineer's work. Detail in docs/comparison.md.
